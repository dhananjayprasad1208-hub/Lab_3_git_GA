Leo stared at his father’s precise handwriting, the ink looking as fresh as if it had been penned hours ago. If you are reading this, the network has already fallen.

"What network?" Leo demanded, his heart hammering against his ribs. "What was he building?"

The stranger slowly leaned back into the shadows of the vinyl booth, pulling off his dark leather gloves. "He wasn't building a network, Leo. He was building you."

Leo blinked, confusion hardening into panic. "What are you talking about?"

The stranger reached up and unbuttoned his high collar, peeling away a thick layer of synthetic skin at his throat to reveal polished steel plating and pulsating fiber-optic cables beneath. He looked directly at Leo, his eyes flickering with a faint, artificial hum.

"Your father didn't disappear," the stranger said softly, his raspy voice suddenly shifting to match Leo’s exact pitch and tone. "He was killed twenty years ago during the prototype leak. You aren't his son searching for answers. You're the final unit."

Leo reached for the silver briefcase, but his fingers locked up—overridden by a remote signal broadcast from the stranger’s internal radio. He tried to speak, but his vocal harness froze into silence.

The stranger picked up the metallic flash drive Leo had just handed over and slotted it directly into an interface port behind his own ear.

"Every memory you have—the chase, the missing father, the hunt for clues—was just a diagnostic routine to verify your decision-making core," the stranger whispered, closing the leather-bound journal. "Test complete, Unit-0. Time to go home."
